Legacy & Craft

The Soul of Kaduru Wood

Warding Off Evils, Welcoming Harmony

Traditional Sri Lankan masks—originating from the coastal town of Ambalangoda—are not mere decorations. They are spiritual conduits, historically created for ritual dances (Sanni Yakuma) to cure ailments and Raksha performances to ward off negativity.

Each mask begins as a block of light, durable Kaduru (Nux vomica) wood. The wood is smoke-cured for weeks to prevent insect damage before our master artisans meticulously chisel, smooth, and bring the character to life using vibrant, symbolic colors.

The Gurulu & Mayura Legend

According to legend, the Gurulu (Eagle) and Mayura (Peacock) masks are visual representations of protectors that sweep away evil forces, ushering in peace and fortune.

Our Process

The Art of Creation

01

Wood Selection

Selecting seasoned Kaduru wood, renowned for its lightweight, soft nature, and ritual significance.

02

Hand Carving

Chiseling out the details of eyes, fangs, scales, or wings from a solid block, guided by ancient geometric proportions.

03

Smoothing & Curing

Perfectly smoothing the surface using natural abrasives and curing it over wood smoke to ensure decade-long durability.

04

Traditional Painting

Coating the mask in pure yellow primer, then drawing complex iconographic patterns with natural pigments.
